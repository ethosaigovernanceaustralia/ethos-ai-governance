ADM Transparency Sprint

From 10 December 2026, your privacy policy has homework.

$1,490 [PRICE TBC] · 10 business days

If a computer program makes decisions about individuals in your business, or does something substantially and directly related to making them, and those decisions could reasonably be expected to significantly affect a person's rights or interests, the Privacy Act will require your privacy policy to say so. That is the effect of Australian Privacy Principles 1.7 to 1.9, introduced by the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 and commencing 10 December 2026.

The obligation is narrower than most commentary suggests. It is a privacy-policy disclosure requirement, not a right to human review of individual decisions. But it comes with real teeth: since December 2024 the OAIC holds expanded enforcement powers, including the ability to issue compliance notices for administrative breaches such as a non-compliant privacy policy, and from 10 December 2026 that power extends to a failure to meet the APP 1.7 transparency requirement specifically.

The hard part is not the drafting. It is working out which of your automated decisions are actually caught. The ADM Transparency Sprint answers that question and produces the disclosure wording, in 10 business days.

Who it is for

  • check_circleAustralian organisations covered by the Privacy Act (APP entities) that use automated decision-making anywhere in their operations: eligibility checks, pricing, credit or risk scoring, application triage, account decisions, content or claim assessments.
  • check_circleAI vendors whose products make or shape decisions about individuals, and who want their own house in order before clients start asking.
  • check_circleCompliance leads who need a defensible written record of how the in-scope analysis was done, not just a paragraph pasted into a policy.

This offer is deliberately open to any Australian APP entity using automated decision-making. You do not need to be an AI company; the obligation attaches to computer-program decision-making generally, of which AI is only one form.

Who it is not for

  • remove_circleOrganisations not covered by the Privacy Act and with no plans to be (for many small businesses, coverage begins once annual turnover exceeds $3 million or an exception to the small-business exemption applies).
  • remove_circleOrganisations that make no automated or partially automated decisions about individuals. The free ADM self-check will tell you in five minutes.
  • remove_circleAnyone seeking legal advice on a contested question of Privacy Act interpretation. Ethos provides governance consulting, not legal advice; where a genuinely contestable legal question surfaces, we will say so and recommend you put it to your lawyer.

What you receive

  1. ADM inventory workshop. A structured working session that walks your team through every point where a computer program makes, or substantially and directly contributes to, a decision about an individual. Output: a documented ADM inventory.
  2. In-scope analysis. Each inventoried decision assessed against the APP 1.7 to 1.9 criteria: is personal information used in the operation of the program, and could the decision reasonably be expected to significantly affect an individual's rights or interests. Output: a decision-by-decision assessment with reasoning recorded.
  3. Drafted privacy-policy disclosure wording. The specified information for your in-scope decisions, drafted in plain language and ready for your privacy policy, structured so your lawyer can review and adopt it efficiently.
  4. Compliance memo. A short written memo recording the methodology, the inventory, the in-scope conclusions and the recommended next steps, so the analysis survives staff turnover and stands up when a client, board or regulator asks how you reached your position.

How it works

  1. Day 1 to 2: intake. You complete a short intake questionnaire and supply your current privacy policy and a description of your systems. No document preparation beyond what already exists.
  2. Day 3 to 5: inventory workshop. One facilitated session (remote, approximately 90 minutes) with the people who know your systems.
  3. Day 6 to 9: analysis and drafting. Ethos completes the in-scope analysis, drafts the disclosure wording and prepares the memo.
  4. Day 10: delivery and walkthrough. All deliverables handed over with a 30-minute walkthrough call and a clear list of what to do next.
