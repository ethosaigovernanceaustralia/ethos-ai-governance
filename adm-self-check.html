ADM Self-Check

Does the new automated decision-making rule apply to you?

A five-minute self-check.

From 10 December 2026, Australian Privacy Principles 1.7 to 1.9 will require organisations covered by the Privacy Act to include specified information in their privacy policy where a computer program makes, or does something substantially and directly related to making, decisions that could reasonably be expected to significantly affect an individual's rights or interests, and personal information is used in the operation of the program.

The obligation catches more than AI. Rules-based software, scoring engines and eligibility checkers can all qualify. It also catches less than the headlines suggest: it is a privacy-policy disclosure requirement, not a right to human review of individual decisions.

Answer the ten questions below about your own operations. Your result gives an indicative view of whether you are likely to need to act, and what the sensible next step is.

info This self-check is a general guide, not legal advice and not a compliance determination. Ethos provides governance consulting, not legal advice. The scoring is indicative only; borderline cases turn on details a questionnaire cannot capture.

The questions

1. Is your organisation covered by the Privacy Act? (Broadly: annual turnover above $3 million, or you trade in personal information, hold a Commonwealth contract or another exception to the small-business exemption applies.)

2. Does any software in your business make decisions about individual people, such as approving, declining, ranking, scoring or prioritising them?

3. Does any software produce recommendations, scores or assessments that a staff member then relies on when deciding about an individual?

4. Do any of these decisions affect what a person can access, receive or do: for example credit, pricing, employment, insurance, housing, benefits, account access or service eligibility?

5. Is personal information about the individual used anywhere in how the software reaches its output?

6. Could an individual reasonably consider one of these decisions significant to their rights or interests, judged from their side of the transaction rather than yours?

7. Do you sell software or AI products that make or shape decisions like these for your clients' end users?

8. Does your current privacy policy say anything at all about automated decision-making?

9. Do you have a written list of where automated decisions happen across your business?

10. Has anyone in your organisation been assigned responsibility for the 10 December 2026 change?

Please answer all ten questions before seeing your result.
